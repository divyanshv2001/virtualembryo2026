I refine the implication for modeling. Causal nonidentifiability does not rule out useful prediction. An eligible gene prior can select a model within an observational equivalence class, and independent, permitted intervention evaluation can support its predictive utility. GEARS explicitly uses coexpression and GO relationships rather than requiring every graph edge to be a demonstrated direct regulator ([full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-023-01905-6)). Its large screens and context requirements also prevent transferring its evidence wholesale to this one-knockout setting. Our claims should distinguish predictive accuracy under a specified distribution from identification of a direct regulatory effect.

True-versus-randomized graphs address a narrower question: do the chosen biological identities contribute beyond matched graph structure and model capacity? Degree-preserving controls do not establish directionality, eliminate genotype-batch confounding, identify direct versus indirect targets, recover omitted regulators, or prove transport to β-catenin. They may preserve some biological structure, and a true graph can carry annotation coverage or cell-state associations rather than causal information. A negative result may reflect sparse panel coverage or poor model optimization. A positive result therefore supports a particular predictive representation, conditional on honest splits and equal tuning; it does not certify regulatory edges.
