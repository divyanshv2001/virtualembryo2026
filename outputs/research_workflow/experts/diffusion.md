# Diffusion and flow models — initial independent review

My worldview is that a generative model earns its complexity by predicting unseen distributions under explicit assumptions. A visually plausible trajectory is not evidence of biological dynamics. Preferred evidence is independent-embryo temporal validation, simple-model comparisons, composition-sensitive metrics, and sensitivity to coupling and growth priors. My field's characteristic failure modes are treating sampling time as developmental time, interpreting a probability-flow ODE as cell motion, and mistaking a fitted bridge for identified lineages.

I read PROJECT_STATE.md and inventory.json first, then inspected the actual diffusion record and prompt. `Neurl IPS 2026/orchestration/personas.py:254–280` asserts that a stationarity pre-test ruled out OT, growth-weighted OT, flow matching and Schrödinger bridges. Its cited files are absent. I explicitly reject that inherited blanket conclusion: there is no accessible test, population definition, uncertainty analysis, or biological dataset with which to audit it. The record is a premise in a persona, not an experiment. Conversely, absence of this evidence does not vindicate any transport model. `Neurl IPS 2026/agenticprompt:206–222` properly asks whether diffusion and flow methods are justified; it does not supply a trained model. `Neurl IPS 2026/agent_orchestrator.py:150–186` evaluates supplied artifacts using a small proxy and unavailable imported modules, while lines 188–197 embed unaudited scores. No architecture or empirical biological finding is demonstrated.

The key hidden assumption is that time-indexed marginal distributions identify a velocity. They do not. Even if a normalized density obeys a continuity equation, different fields can induce the same density evolution; finitely many snapshots constrain still less. A static OT cost chooses one coupling through a minimal-displacement prior. A Schrödinger bridge selects paths relative to a reference stochastic process. Those choices can be useful prediction regularizers, but neither creates experimentally observed ancestors. Fitting endpoint distributions is interpolation. Extrapolation additionally assumes how the vector field, growth law and conditioning behave outside observed stages. A failed autonomous constant-velocity model would not logically falsify stage-conditioned or growth-aware transport. Similarly, near-stationary expression marginals can conceal cell turnover or spatial motion.

Primary literature supports methods, not project performance. [TrajectoryNet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8320749/) (Tong et al., ICML/PMLR 119, 2020; published; full text inspected) regularizes continuous flows with energy, density, velocity and growth information and evaluates held-out intermediate distributions. It provides a concrete example of assumptions that constrain paths; its interpolation success is not an extrapolation guarantee. [Simulation-Free Schrödinger Bridges via Score and Flow Matching](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/tong24a/tong24a.pdf) (Tong et al., AISTATS/PMLR 238, 2024; published; full text inspected) learns score and flow fields using entropy-regularized transport and discusses ODE/SDE paths sharing marginals. This directly cautions against uniquely interpreting sampled paths. [Joint Velocity-Growth Flow Matching for Single-Cell Dynamics Modeling](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/eb1bad7a84ef68a64f1afd6577725d45-Abstract-Conference.html) (Wang et al., NeurIPS 38, 2025; published conference paper, DOI 10.52202/085713-5363; abstract only verified) jointly models state and mass using semi-relaxed OT. The abstract establishes relevance to unbalanced populations; I have not independently audited its reported superiority or proofs. Three search queries were used.

Growth deserves special care. Measured cells per snapshot reflect capture effort as well as proliferation/death. Normalizing each snapshot to unit mass removes absolute abundance information. A growth-aware model must distinguish independently measured population size, sampling fraction, and relative cell-state composition; otherwise its growth head can fit observation artifacts. The [official T2 scoring page](https://virtualembryo.ai/challenge/evaluation?section=scoring&task=2) separately measures expression, distribution, physical shape/scale and neighbourhood profiles, and permits different predicted cell counts. Predicted sample count therefore cannot itself establish biological growth. Expression transport alone also cannot justify geometry or local spatial organisation.

Concrete improvement: prepare a preregistered benchmark with copy-last, empirical mixture resampling with stage-dependent weights, and a regularized linear latent trend before conditional flow escalation. Use the same permitted training stages, training-only representation, cell sampling budget and tuning budget. Evaluate leave-one-stage interpolation separately from rolling-origin extrapolation, grouped by embryo; never randomly split cells as independent temporal replicates. Ablate stage conditioning, coupling regularization, and independently supported growth priors. Report raw de_score, de_direction, mmd_u, variogram and T2 spatial metrics, with embryo-level uncertainty and repeated sampling. Any gain remains a hypothesis until data arrive.

Strengths are the prompt's explicit skepticism and the orchestration's ability to preserve falsifiable proposals. Weaknesses are absent data, absent biological implementation, and historical scores substituting for auditable results. I decline an immediate high-dimensional score-SDE implementation: there is no demonstrated failure of simpler conditioned distribution models to justify its cost. Confidence is high in the evidence-status and nonidentifiability diagnosis; moderate in the benchmark's usefulness; low in any predicted architecture advantage.
