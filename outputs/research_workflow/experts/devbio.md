# Computational developmental biology — initial review

My worldview is that a developmental prediction must specify what evolves: population abundance, molecular state within a population, tissue organization, or actual ancestry. These are different observables. I prefer independent embryos, stage metadata tied to morphology where available, concordant marker programs, and interventions or tracing for mechanism. My main failure modes are treating annotation changes as population extinction, interpreting capture fractions as proliferation, and imposing smooth time on a developmental event. The persona in Neurl IPS 2026/orchestration/personas.py:55–84 usefully foregrounds these concerns; its autobiographical claims and absent mandate files provide no evidence.

I read PROJECT_STATE.md and inventory.json first. No biological data, trained model, predictions or experiment logs exist. Neurl IPS 2026/prompts.md:329–335 lists abundance, conditioned expression, lineage transition and trajectory together, but the latter two require additional assumptions or observations. Neurl IPS 2026/prompts.md:1193 and :1196 frame smooth latent development and state conditioning as hypotheses. They must remain hypotheses, even when a generated developmental movie appears plausible.

The existing evaluator contains sensible submission checks for ordered panels, nonnegative finite expression and coordinates (Neurl IPS 2026/agent_orchestrator.py:128–147). This is a strength in artifact hygiene, not biological validity. Its stage proxy (same file:150–186) uses the first at most 150 cells of candidates and references, without embryo grouping or randomized repeated sampling. A file ordering enriched for one population can therefore change a diagnostic result. The hardcoded score dictionary at :188–196 is not an experiment record, despite its comment. Missing input files become empty text in Neurl IPS 2026/orchestration/perceive.py:63–67; absence cannot be treated as an empirical failure.

Two primary studies clarify the inferential boundary. Schiebinger et al., Cell (2019), published research, [full text verified](https://www.broadinstitute.org/files/publications/2019/03/1-s2.0-S009286741930039X-main.pdf), DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2019.01.006, infer probabilistic developmental couplings with explicit short-step and Markov assumptions while allowing growth/death. Their dense reprogramming time course establishes a method and assumptions, not embryo performance here. Weinreb et al., Science (2020), published research, [abstract verified](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31974159/), DOI 10.1126/science.aaw3381, use clonal barcodes and identify fate biases hidden from expression measurements. PMC access failed, so I do not claim full-text verification. This supports caution about expression-only fate inference; it does not establish identical limitations quantitatively in embryos.

I propose a minimal biological comparison before architecture escalation: estimate population weights separately from conditional expression distributions. Fit shared broad state definitions using training data only; retain soft membership and an unknown-state category. Compare nearest-stage resampling, training-only temporal reweighting with frozen conditional expression, and reweighting plus conditional expression changes. Test whether reweighting alone lowers mmd_u at a held-out stage. Also inspect de_direction to determine whether apparent aggregate changes come from population weights or within-state expression. This decomposition is a diagnostic convention, not a causal allocation: different state definitions can change the answer.

Hidden assumptions are that states remain recognizable across stages, capture efficiency is sufficiently comparable, training samples contain support for target populations, and stage ordering is biologically informative. New cell states may invalidate reweighting. Morphological metadata, if supplied, should test whether nominal day explains residual errors; I will not invent somite counts. Annotation harmonization must distinguish renamed populations from genuinely absent marker programs.

Validation requires whole-embryo splits where identifiers exist, training-only preprocessing, matched cell counts, fixed panels, repeated random sampling and embryo-level uncertainty. With only one embryo per stage, report descriptive stage prediction and explicitly withhold population-level generalization. A negative held-out mmd_u difference under the preregistered sampling analysis kills the reweighting-improvement hypothesis; disappearance after accounting for capture or annotation changes weakens its developmental interpretation. No tests are presently executable because data and scoring dependencies are absent.

I reject adding a hard developmental lineage tree to make generated cells appear biologically coherent. Marginal snapshots cannot uniquely identify ancestry, and a tree can erase convergence or unsupported new states. Confidence is high in the need for the inferential distinction and moderate in the usefulness of the proposed comparison; confidence in a score gain is low until tested.

Questions for sysbio: what independent evidence separates changed population fractions from proliferation? For diffusion: can your generator represent a newly appearing state without relabeling an old one, and what held-out test establishes this? For spatial3d: how will tissue rearrangement be distinguished from specimen alignment and scale?
