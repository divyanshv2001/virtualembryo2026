# genmodel — initial review, 2026-09-27

My worldview is that a generator earns its complexity by predicting a specified observable distribution better than a strong sampler. Sample realism, reconstruction loss, and a convincing developmental narrative are different claims. My preferred evidence is a reproducible likelihood contract, independent-embryo predictive checks, and decomposition of distributional error into composition and within-population state. Conditional generation is not a mechanistic simulator: matching snapshots neither identifies cell ancestry nor the causal dynamics connecting them.

For Task 1 I would define the prediction as the distribution of measured, normalised expression for a randomly sampled cell from the target dissection, conditional on permitted training measurements and target stage. A useful decomposition is p(x|t,a)=sum_c pi(c|t,a) p(x|c,t,a), where a records the observation process. This exposes separate hypotheses about changing population proportions and changing expression within populations. Assay and dissection effects must remain explicit rather than being attributed automatically to development. If an annotation is unavailable at prediction time, its value is a generated latent label, not an input supplied by the answer.

The [official data page](https://virtualembryo.ai/challenge/data), inspected as full webpage, specifies log1p-normalised Task-1 X, independent predicted cell identities, a frozen typing classifier, and distributional scoring. It also warns that labels are not harmonised and that dissection changes population coverage. Thus raw-count likelihoods cannot simply be applied to X. The [rules](https://virtualembryo.ai/challenge/rules), inspected as full webpage, permit disclosed external resources subject to protected stage/genotype windows. This corrects historical blanket prohibitions; pretrained models need provenance checks too. The task-specific evaluation URL failed twice, so I have not verified the persona's target-fitted 30-PC claim. That detail must be confirmed against the actual starter scorer before optimisation.

Relevant primary literature is Lopez et al., “Deep generative modeling for single-cell transcriptomics,” Nature Methods, 2018, published version, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-018-0229-2). Its abstract and an indexed full-text excerpt establish explicit nuisance modelling and a ZINB observation model; direct PMC retrieval hit a browser challenge, so complete methods verification remains pending. Lotfollahi et al., “scGen predicts single-cell perturbation responses,” Nature Methods, 2019, published version, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0494-8), was verified at abstract level, with visible supplementary captions but paywalled main text. It motivates latent response transfer in tested settings, not unrestricted transfer to new genotypes or entirely absent developmental populations. These are prior methods, not evidence this project works.

The strongest feature of the supplied framework is its explicit falsifier requirement. Its main weakness for my discipline is omission of observation support. A categorical cell-type decoder cannot generate a genuinely unseen category without an explicit representation or fallback. A continuous decoder can emit a novel-looking profile, but that alone does not validate a new type. Report unsupported populations separately; distinguish label renaming from missing biological support. Do not force all new cells into existing annotation names.

Concrete improvement: freeze a planned factorial benchmark with last-stage resampling, composition-only reweighting, within-type state shifts, and their combination before a conditional VAE. Estimate weights using training data only. Hold sampling, preprocessing, and scorer fixed. Compare mmd_u and differential-expression metrics alongside per-type moments, covariance, and type proportions; report population-conditional error where reference labels permit it. Fit a count decoder only to verified raw counts, otherwise use a justified continuous observation model for normalised values. Validate observation support, zero frequency, variance, and library-size dependence before temporal generalisation. Distinguish decoder sampling variability from uncertainty about future stage and composition, using embryo-level refits when replication exists. No intervals are calibrated yet.

I explicitly reject starting with latent diffusion because its expressive samples cannot repair unsupported future cell types or an undefined likelihood. Confidence is high in the need for this contract and moderate that the decomposition improves diagnosis; confidence in any score gain is low because data and scorer are absent. Questions for diffusion: what nonstationary extrapolation assumption will be falsified? For neuralode: what observable prediction survives nonidentifiable trajectories? For replearn: can a latent decoder improve within-type distributions after composition is held fixed?
